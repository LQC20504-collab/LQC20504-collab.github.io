The search of Prospect Garden (大观园) is the great watershed of *Dream of the Red Chamber*.

Before it, the garden is a world of youth: poetry clubs, flower burials, butterfly chasing, couplet contests, quarrels that still carry the lightness of childhood. After it, the rooms empty out one by one — Qingwen dies, Siqi is expelled, Ruihua is dismissed, Baochai moves away — until the place is too desolate to return to.

Most readers lay the blame on Lady Wang and call her the executioner of feudal parenthood. The verdict is not wrong, but it is too comfortable. The question worth asking is harder: **why did she order the search? What exactly was she afraid of?**

## 1. The spark: one embroidered sachet

In Chapter 73, the simple-minded maid is digging for crickets behind a rockery in Prospect Garden when she finds a five-colored embroidered sachet. The workmanship is exquisite and genuinely charming — except that what is embroidered on it is not flowers or birds. On one side, two naked figures locked in an embrace. On the other, a few characters.

The maid does not understand what she is holding. Smiling, she carries it along and runs straight into Lady Xing — who, "frightened out of her wits, clutches it tight," swears her to silence, and then sends her own serving woman, the wife of Wang Shanbao, to seal the sachet and deliver it to Lady Wang.

That is the entire immediate cause. One forbidden object, one ignorant girl, one hand-off made in bad faith.

## 2. Lady Wang's first reaction is not to investigate. It is fear

When the sachet reaches her, Lady Wang's first move is not to find out whose it is. It is to be "frightened to death."

She suspects Wang Xifeng first. In tears, she asks her: *"How did this thing come to be left in our garden?"* Xifeng turns pale and kneels to defend herself.

The detail matters: what terrifies Lady Wang is not licentiousness as such. It is that **this object was found inside Prospect Garden, which means she, the mistress of the household, has failed at her job.**

For Lady Wang, the sachet is not a moral problem. It is a problem of authority. She needs a swift, visible action to prove that she still runs this house.

## 3. The real fuse was laid back in Chapter 34

After Baoyu is beaten, Lady Wang summons Xiren for questioning. Xiren says this:

> "Master Bao is grown up now, and the young ladies inside are grown up too. And Miss Lin and Miss Bao are his cousins on his aunts' sides. Sisters they may be, but they are still men and women. To sit and stand together day and night is not convenient, and one cannot help worrying."

And then the suggestion: *"There ought to be some way of arranging things so that Master Bao moves out of the garden to live."*

There are two calculations in that sentence — clearing herself of responsibility, and drawing a line around the garden as dangerous ground. Lady Wang's reaction is that she feels "as if struck by thunder," and her affection for Xiren redoubles on the spot; she raises Xiren's allowance to the level of a concubine that very day.

From that moment, "the garden is not clean" becomes a time bomb in Lady Wang's mind. The sachet is merely the match.

## 4. Lady Xing's arithmetic: one family disgrace, two uses

Lady Xing is the wife of the senior branch, yet the household is run by Lady Wang of the junior branch. She has swallowed that insult for years.

The sachet, in her hands, is both a disgrace and a weapon. Handing it to Lady Wang to deal with does three things at once: it makes her look magnanimous; it transfers the difficulty *and* the blame; and whatever Lady Wang does, Lady Xing gets to stand at the side and watch.

And into this gap steps Wang Shanbao's wife with a piece of slander that does enormous damage. Her target is Qingwen:

> "That girl relies on being prettier than the rest, and on having a sharp tongue. She dresses up like Xi Shi every day, and in front of people she talks as if she owned the place, always pushing to be first."

Lady Wang hears this and "something stirs in her memory" — she recalls once seeing Qingwen scold a junior maid in the garden, and thinks: *"I never could stand that brazen look of hers."*

Note the order of events. **The sachet belonged to Siqi, and Lady Wang never learns this. But Qingwen's fate is settled before the search even begins.** The search looks like it is about objects. It is actually about people, and the accounts against those people were written up somewhere else entirely.

## 5. Lady Wang's three layers of anxiety

Take her motive apart and three fears are stacked on top of one another.

**The first is a mother's fear.** She lost her elder son, Jia Zhu. Baoyu is her only remaining hope. She genuinely believes the girls in the garden will "seduce him and ruin him." Her words to Qingwen: *"If anything happens to my good Baoyu, if he is led astray by this creature, what then?"*

**The second is the anxiety of a mistress losing her grip.** If word of the sachet reaches the Dowager, Lady Wang has no answer. The search is how she proves to her elders, her sister-in-law, and the whole household that she is still in charge.

**The third is the collective panic of a declining house.** By this point the Jia family is already sliding. At the Mid-Autumn banquet the Dowager herself laments that these are no longer the prosperous days. The search is not a display of confidence. It is a display of the lack of it. A family with real standing does not need to turn out every maid's chest to prove it has nothing to hide.

One more thing deserves notice: Lady Wang was not always this "wooden figure." Granny Liu remembers her as "brisk and quick, good with people, and never putting on airs." The Dowager's verdict is different: *"Poor thing — she hardly speaks, she is like a block of wood."*

A woman who was once brisk has become wood. Something pressed her into that shape. She upgraded fears she could not process into morality, and then handed that morality over to violence.

## 6. How "good intentions" become violence

The conduct of the search is the clearest evidence.

Wang Xifeng is in fact against it. She proposes: *"If we search, let us search only our own people. Miss Bao's rooms must on no account be searched."* She even wants to use the occasion to clear out the older and more troublesome maids by marrying them off.

There is a crucial division of labor here: **Xifeng is thinking about downsizing. Lady Wang is thinking about disinfection.** Downsizing weighs costs and benefits. Disinfection only wants thoroughness.

The results are well known. Siqi is found with love tokens from her cousin Pan Youan and is expelled. Ruihua, Xichun's maid, is found with money her brother had left in her keeping; Xichun cuts herself off from the Ning household entirely and drives Ruihua out, saying, *"I am a clean, pure person — why should you drag me down with you?"* Qingwen is driven out in Chapter 77 and dies a few days later.

Of all these girls, exactly one is "guilty" — Siqi — and her crime is that she fell in love. The rest are simply crushed in passing by an operation carried out in the name of protection.

## 7. Tanchun's slap, and her speech

When the search reaches Tanchun's quarters, she is "waiting at the open door with a candle in her hand." She says:

> "Naturally our maids are all thieves, and I am the chief receiver of stolen goods. In that case, search my chests first. Anything they have stolen, I will have hidden it for them."

Wang Shanbao's wife, tactless, steps forward and lifts the edge of Tanchun's jacket, saying she has now even searched the young lady's own person. Tanchun slaps her across the face: *"Who do you think you are, to lay hands on my clothes?"*

But the important part is what she says next — one of the great verdicts in the whole novel:

> "Don't be in such a hurry. Your own turn to be searched will come. Did you not notice this morning that the Zhen family had their house searched? Their own house, searched — and today it has happened to us as well. We are coming to it too, by degrees. Understand this: a great clan like ours cannot be destroyed from outside, not for a long time. As the old saying goes, a hundred-legged insect does not stiffen in death. It must first destroy itself from within before it can be brought down utterly."

And as she speaks, "the tears come despite herself."

Tanchun is one of the few people in the Jia household who can see a structural crisis. What she says is not a tantrum. It is a prophecy: the search of Prospect Garden is not a rectification. It is the beginning of the family's self-destruction — not defeated by others, but by its own hand.

## Conclusion

Why did Lady Wang order the search of Prospect Garden?

Because she loved her son. Because she felt responsible for the household. Because a single embroidered sachet frightened her. Because she was used as someone else's weapon. Because the whole family was sinking behind her back, and she could find no other way to prove she was still of use.

Her tragedy is this: **her motive contained love, but that love had no capacity to understand.** She could not tell youth from lewdness, could not tell a girl's vitality from a "vixen," could not tell running a household from destroying one. She did the worst thing for the best reason available.

And Cao Xueqin's attitude toward her is not simple condemnation. He makes Lady Wang a **part** of the tragedy, not its cause. The cause is the order that forces people to "first destroy themselves from within." Tanchun saw it, and wept. Lady Wang did not see it, and acted.
